    The heart of everything

    Jesus Christ — guide, Saviour and friend

    Before any doctrine, any church and any tradition, there is a Person. Everything we believe grows out of what He did for us. If you take away only one thing from this page, let it be Him.

    01

    God already loves you

    Not the version of you that has everything together — you, today, exactly as you are. His love came first, before you ever looked for Him.

    How the Bible puts it Jeremiah 31:3 · 1 John 4:10
    02

    Something is broken, and we know it

    Every one of us has failed, hurt someone, and felt the distance. The Bible calls it sin. Naming it honestly is the first step towards being healed of it.

    How the Bible puts it Romans 3:23 · Isaiah 59:2
    03

    Jesus paid what you could not

    He lived the life we could not live and died the death we deserved — then rose again. Salvation is a gift you receive, never a wage you earn.

    How the Bible puts it Ephesians 2:8-9 · John 3:16
    04

    He is coming back for you

    This world is not the end of the story. Jesus promised to return, wipe away every tear and make all things new. That hope is why we are called Adventists.

    How the Bible puts it John 14:1-3 · Revelation 21:4
    05

    Your part is small, and it is yours

    Four things. Not one of them is a ceremony, and nobody has to be watching.

    1. Tell God the truth about yourself.
    2. Ask Him to forgive you.
    3. Let Him have the say over your life from today.
    4. Thank Him.

    Do not do it alone. Tell one person.

    How the Bible puts it 1 John 1:9 · John 1:12

    What we believe

    Simple answers to honest questions

    Seventh-day Adventists hold 28 fundamental beliefs, all drawn from the Bible. Here are the ones people ask us about most — in plain words, with the texts so you can check them yourself.

    01Where do your beliefs come from?
    From the Bible alone. We do not ask anyone to accept something because a church says so — we ask you to read it for yourself and decide before God. If we ever teach something Scripture does not, we are the ones who need to change.2 Timothy 3:16-17 · Acts 17:11
    02How is a person saved?
    By grace, through faith in Jesus — never by being good enough. Obedience is the fruit of salvation, never its price. You cannot buy what He already paid for.Ephesians 2:8-9 · Titus 3:5
    03Why do you worship on Saturday?
    Because God rested on the seventh day at creation and wrote it into the Ten Commandments, and Jesus Himself kept it. The Sabbath is not a burden — it is a weekly gift of rest, family and worship from Friday sunset to Saturday sunset.Genesis 2:1-3 · Exodus 20:8-11 · Luke 4:16
    04What happens when someone dies?
    The Bible calls death a sleep — a rest without pain or fear — until Jesus returns and calls His people back to life. It means your loved ones are not suffering, and it means the reunion is real.Ecclesiastes 9:5 · John 11:11-14 · 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17
    05What is the second coming?
    The literal, visible, personal return of Jesus to this earth — seen by everyone, not a secret event. No one knows the day or the hour, and anyone who sets a date is speaking beyond Scripture.Acts 1:11 · Matthew 24:36 · Revelation 1:7
    06Why do Adventists care so much about health?
    Because your body is God's temple, and a clear mind hears Him better. Rest, water, sunlight, exercise, good food and trust in God are not rules to earn heaven — they are kindness to the body He gave you.1 Corinthians 6:19-20 · 3 John 1:2
    07What is baptism, and do I need it?
    It is a public "yes" to Jesus — going down into the water as He did, picturing a death to the old life and a rising to a new one. It is a decision you make freely, when you understand it and want it. No one will ever push you.Matthew 28:19-20 · Romans 6:3-4
    08Do I have to change my life before I come?
    No. Come exactly as you are. Jesus never asked anyone to clean themselves up first — He accepted people, and the change followed from being loved. Let Him do the work; He is better at it than we are.Matthew 11:28 · Romans 5:8
    The seventh day

    A day God gave away

    Once a week, from sunset on Friday to sunset on Saturday, we stop. Not because stopping earns us anything, but because Someone who loves us set the time aside and asked us to spend it with Him.

    When it begins where you live

    In the Bible a day runs from evening to evening, and evening is when the sun goes down. So the Sabbath begins at sunset on Friday and ends at sunset on Saturday.

    That means the day starts at a slightly different moment in Pensacola than in Key West. Choose your city and you will see this week's times for your part of the state.

    A gift you did not have to earn

    If you take one thing from this page, take this: the Sabbath is not how anyone gets saved.

    Rest came before work

    Genesis puts the first Sabbath at the very end of the creation week, before a single day of human work is recorded.

    Genesis 2:1-3

    Freedom came before the commandment

    When God gave Israel the Ten Commandments, they were already free. Grace has always come first.

    Deuteronomy 5:12-15

    Made for you, not against you

    Jesus said the Sabbath was made for people, not people for the Sabbath.

    Mark 2:27-28 · Matthew 12:1-12

    So we do not keep this day in order to be accepted. We keep it because we already are.

    Why the seventh day

    This is our answer, given the way we would give it over coffee. It is not aimed at anyone.

    It was there before anyone was religious

    Before there was a temple, a nation or a church to belong to, there was a day God stopped on, blessed, and set aside.

    Genesis 2:1-3

    The one commandment that begins “Remember”

    God asks you to remember something: a day, a Maker, and the fact that you are not a machine.

    Exodus 20:8-11

    It was Jesus' own habit

    Luke says going to worship on the Sabbath was Jesus' custom. He spent those hours healing people and doing good.

    Luke 4:16 · Mark 2:28

    The first Christians kept it too

    Luke's account of the early church shows Paul in the synagogue on the Sabbath week after week. We are not claiming more than the text says.

    Luke 23:56 · Acts 13:42-44 · Acts 17:2 · Acts 18:4
    What a Sabbath actually looks like

    Nobody hands you a schedule when you arrive, and no two families do this identically.

    Friday afternoon

    Called the preparation day, and it earns the name.

    Half an hour before sunset

    Work stops. Phones go down. Somebody puts a cloth on the table.

    Sunset

    The family gathers — or the two of you, or just you and an open Bible.

    Friday evening

    A simple meal cooked earlier, and no screens.

    Saturday morning · Bible study

    Everyone sings together for a few minutes, then people split into small groups.

    Late morning · The main service

    Roughly seventy-five to ninety minutes.

    Lunch together

    On many Sabbaths everybody stays for a shared meal.

    Saturday afternoon, until sunset

    The quietest and, for a lot of us, the best part.

    Under all of it there is one idea: for twenty-four hours, nothing is owed.

    Honest questions

    These are the ones we get asked most often.

    01Do I have to keep the Sabbath to be saved?
    No. You are saved by trusting Jesus.Ephesians 2:8-9 · Romans 3:28
    02What about Christians who worship on Sunday?
    They are our brothers and sisters, and we are not their judges.Romans 14:4 · Acts 17:11 · John 13:35
    03So what do you actually do — and not do?
    Most of us let the ordinary business of the week go for those hours.Isaiah 58:13-14 · Matthew 12:12
    04My job needs me on Saturdays. Does that shut me out?
    Not at all, and please do not let it stop you from coming.
    05My family will not like this. What then?
    Then go slowly.

    Frequently asked

    Before you write to us

    ?Is everything really free?
    Yes. Bible studies, the books, the visits and the prayers cost you nothing, ever. We will never ask you for money — not at the first study and not at the last one.
    ?Do I have to become an Adventist?
    No. Many people study with us for years and stay in their own church, or in none. Our goal is that you meet Jesus. What you do after that is between you and Him.
    ?I live far from Delray Beach. Can you still help?
    Yes — we serve the whole state, from Pensacola to Key West. If no missionary is near your town, we will study with you by video call and put you in touch with the nearest congregation.
    ?What if I do not speak English well?
    Write to us in your own language. We work in English, Spanish, Haitian Creole, French, Portuguese, German, Dutch, Russian and Ukrainian, and we will find someone who speaks yours.
    ?I have doubts and hard questions. Is that a problem?
    Not at all — bring them. Faith that has never been questioned is not very strong. We would rather talk honestly about a hard question than pretend it does not exist.
    ?Are you the official Adventist Church?
    We are an independent group of lay members who support the work of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. We are not a conference office and we do not speak for the denomination — we simply share the same faith and point people to a local church.
